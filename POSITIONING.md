# Exposure Intelligence — Positioning

> Layer: go-to-market
> Version: 1.0
> Audience: product, commercial, partner conversations

---

## 1. Category framing

**What this is:**
Exposure intelligence infrastructure — a standardized technical evidence artifact that sits beneath legal advisory on AI risk.

**What this is not:**
- AI governance consulting
- A compliance SaaS platform
- A policy template vendor
- A workshop offering
- A generic risk dashboard

The distinction matters in every partner conversation. "AI governance" is already a crowded, abstract category. Exposure intelligence is a narrower, more precise claim: structured technical evidence that a legal advisor can use immediately, under their own name, without creating a data-processing surface.

---

## 2. Core doctrine

**The artifact surfaces conditions. The lawyer names the law.**

This is the anti-disintermediation rule. It governs every section of every artifact.

The artifact describes technical and organizational conditions — what is deployed, what does not exist, what cannot be excluded, what requires verification. It does not cite statutes, interpret provisions, or draw legal conclusions. The lawyer reads a condition and their own legal knowledge activates. That activation is the billable moment.

Violations of this rule reduce the law firm's value and make the artifact look like a legal memo generator rather than a technical evidence instrument.

---

## 3. The product in one sentence

> A structured technical AI risk evidence pack your legal advisor can use under their name, without creating a data-processing surface.

---

## 4. What the artifact is and is not

**Is:**
- Technical and organizational exposure indicators
- Vendor dependency and reversibility analysis
- Evidence gaps that require legal review
- Escalation signals for attorney engagement

**Is not:**
- Legal advice
- A compliance certification
- An audit
- A legal opinion on liability

The lawyer interprets. The artifact provides the evidence layer.

This distinction is what makes law-firm distribution structurally viable: the artifact does not compete with the attorney's role. It extends it.

---

## 5. Distribution thesis

Primary channel: boutique law firms, white-label, under their letterhead.

Why law firms:
- They hold the client relationship and billing trust
- AI risk questions are already arriving in their inboxes
- They cannot build the technical evidence layer themselves at reasonable cost
- They can embed this artifact into legal opinions, due diligence memos, and client-facing risk reviews without software procurement

Why boutique over large firm:
- Boutiques move faster on new practice areas
- Less internal gatekeeping around third-party tools
- More likely to own the client relationship end-to-end
- Target jurisdictions (France, Switzerland, Luxembourg, Belgium) are boutique-strong markets for corporate and digital law

Why white-label:
- The artifact is credible under the law firm's name
- The engine behind it is not the selling point — the evidence pack is
- Proprietary methodology stays proprietary

## 6. Anti-consulting guardrails

The product must not drift into consulting.

| Consulting drift | Infrastructure anchor |
|---|---|
| "We'll customize this for your client" | Same archetype → same artifact shape. No per-client customization in v1. |
| "We'll run workshops to gather input" | Input is an archetype intake form. No discovery sessions. |
| "We'll give recommendations" | The artifact produces pathways and escalation signals. The lawyer recommends. |
| "We'll do ongoing governance" | The artifact is a point-in-time evidence pack. Ongoing retainer is a v2 offer. |
| "Let us explain our methodology" | The methodology is invisible. The artifact is what ships. |

If a law firm partner asks how it works:

> "We run a structured assessment against a client archetype — sector, jurisdiction, company size, AI surface — and produce a standardized evidence pack. The methodology is proprietary. What you receive is the artifact."

---

## 7. Lawyer meeting framing

**Opening:**

> "I'm not trying to replace the legal review. I'm building the technical evidence layer underneath it. The first version doesn't ingest client data — it runs on a client archetype and produces a standardized exposure artifact you could use under your name with clients."

**Qualifying questions:**

1. "Are clients already asking you AI-risk questions?"
2. "Would you bill a structured technical evidence artifact under your name in the next 90 days?"
3. "Which client profile would ask first?"
4. "Would this be more useful as a diagnostic, a due-diligence module, or a lead-generation scan?"
5. "What wording would make this credible to a board or general counsel?"

**The meeting test:**

> "Would you bill this under your name to a client in the next 90 days?"

If yes: build pilot.
If no: use the lawyer for language validation, not distribution.

The meeting is not won by the product being impressive. It is won by the lawyer seeing a client they already have who would buy this tomorrow.

---

## 8. Roadmap shape

**v1 — Pilot artifact business:**
One artifact template. Four starter archetypes. One demo-ready sample. White-label law firm distribution.

**v2 — Transaction module:**
AI Exposure Diligence Pack for M&A and investment contexts. Named-entity structural analysis. Higher price band (€15k–€100k+).

**v3 — Partner infrastructure:**
Annual partner access. Intake form → artifact generation. Branded delivery tooling.

Build v3 only after v1 has commercial pull. Do not engineer the platform before the artifact earns its price.
