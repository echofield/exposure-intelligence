# Opportunity Archetypes

> Layer: strategy and offer design
> Version: 1.0
> Audience: internal product, law-firm partner conversations, demo prioritization

---

## Thesis

The strongest opportunities for Exposure Intelligence are not generic "AI governance" use cases. They are asymmetric legal architectures: markets where regulatory constraints, procurement rules, vendor dependencies, and evidence burdens can be turned into commercial advantage.

This document ranks three premium archetypes:

1. French construction procurement moat
2. Sovereign SaaS / SREN / DORA moat
3. PE AI diligence / vendor dependency moat

Each archetype is valuable because the buyer does not merely want to "be compliant." The buyer wants to win tenders, reduce procurement friction, preserve valuation, pass diligence, improve bargaining power, or enter markets that weaker competitors cannot credibly access.

Exposure Intelligence should frame these as evidence and structuring products used by legal advisors, not as standalone legal advice. The artifact surfaces conditions. The lawyer names the law.

---

## Ranking

| Rank | Archetype | Primary buyer | Main value driver | Premium potential |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | French construction procurement moat | French construction group bidding on public tenders | Higher win probability through evidence, traceability, and tender defensibility | Very high |
| 2 | Sovereign SaaS / SREN / DORA moat | European B2B SaaS selling into public sector, health, or finance | Access to restricted buyers through sovereignty, reversibility, and operator-role discipline | Very high |
| 3 | PE AI diligence / vendor dependency moat | PE sponsor, M&A lawyer, portfolio operator | Valuation protection through hidden AI exposure and vendor dependency discovery | High |

The construction archetype is the cleanest demo because the business payoff is immediate and legible: better tenders, lower buyer risk, stronger award narrative, and higher negotiation leverage. The SaaS sovereignty archetype is more technically sophisticated and more powerful for technology buyers. The PE diligence archetype is the transaction wedge and can become the higher-ticket module once the artifact has market credibility.

## 1. French Construction Procurement Moat

### Archetype Definition

A mid-market or upper-mid-market French construction group, design-build firm, civil works operator, or infrastructure contractor that regularly competes for municipal, hospital, transport, education, and state infrastructure tenders.

The opportunity is not generic sustainability compliance. The opportunity is to turn procurement documentation, environmental proof, lifecycle evidence, supply-chain traceability, and contract architecture into a repeatable tender advantage.

France is attractive because public procurement is a major economic lever and environmental criteria are being embedded more deeply into award and execution logic. The OECD has identified strategic and green public procurement as a major French policy direction, and commentary on French procurement reform points to environmental award criteria becoming structurally more important from 21 August 2026. Sources: [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/promoting-strategic-and-green-public-procurement-in-france_70da2048-en.html), [Bird & Bird](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2022/france/environmental-aspects-will-be-considered-by-french-public-purchasers-when-assessing-the-various-bids).

### Constraint Stack

| Constraint | Why it matters commercially |
|---|---|
| Environmental award criteria | Bids need defensible environmental evidence, not generic ESG wording. |
| Circular procurement and sourcing expectations | Material provenance, reuse, recycled content, and low-carbon sourcing become bid inputs. |
| Public buyer review burden | Buyers prefer offers that are easier to evaluate, justify, and defend. |
| Supplier fragility | Single-source low-carbon inputs can weaken delivery credibility. |
| Documentation latency | Weak competitors assemble proof too late or inconsistently. |

### Structural Weakness In The Market

Most contractors treat sustainable procurement documentation as overhead. They collect supplier emails, generic attestations, recycled-content claims, product sheets, and environmental copy in an ad hoc way during the tender sprint.

That weak structure creates three problems:

- The bid team cannot assemble evidence quickly.
- The public buyer cannot easily defend the award internally.
- The contractor cannot credibly show fallback options if a supplier fails.

This is where the moat appears. The contractor with a standing evidence system becomes easier to award to.

### Leverage Points

**Procurement defensibility:** Build a standing tender file by segment: lifecycle data, material provenance, subcontractor attestations, environmental response templates, and supporting evidence by material class.

**Supply-chain traceability:** Replace single-supplier credibility with a multi-source approved-vendor framework, documented equivalence standards, and substitute proof packs.

**Contracting leverage:** Maintain a clause library covering substitution, evidentiary cooperation, acceptance testing, change orders, environmental proof obligations, and downstream subcontractor flow-down duties.

**Buyer risk reduction:** Translate the evidence posture into an award narrative that makes the public buyer's decision easier to justify.

### Opportunity Architecture

| Dimension | Baseline weak structure | Stronger architecture | Strategic upside | Tradeoff |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tender evidence | Ad hoc sustainability claims and scattered supplier documents | Standing tender evidence pack by project type and material class | Faster bid assembly, lower rejection risk, stronger award narrative | Higher front-loaded discipline |
| Material sourcing | Preferred suppliers with limited substitutes | Multi-source vendor framework with documented equivalence standards | More credible commitments and stronger supplier negotiation | More qualification work |
| Contract terms | Tender terms accepted with minimal tailoring | Clause library for evidence, substitution, audit cooperation, and change orders | Better margin protection and lower dispute risk | Requires legal maintenance |
| Digital tools | Estimation, BIM, or supplier scoring used informally | Governed logs, human review points, data minimization, and system documentation | Stronger board and buyer confidence | Slower deployment |

### Premium Artifact Shape

The sellable artifact is a procurement intelligence dossier, not a compliance report.

Core sections:

- Tender defensibility map
- Mandatory evidence checklist by tender segment
- Supplier dependency and substitution map
- Environmental response model answers
- Clause library for evidence cooperation and substitution
- Buyer-facing award narrative
- Board summary of where structure creates bid advantage

The artifact should show three layers: what the procurement environment requires, where buyer discretion sits, and how the contractor can make its offer easier to defend.

### Monetization Angle

This can be sold by a boutique law firm or legal-technical advisory partner as a premium tender-strategy structuring product.

Indicative pricing hypothesis:

| Product | Range |
|---|---|
| 2-4 week procurement evidence diagnostic | EUR 15k-35k |
| Board-ready structuring memo and tender roadmap | EUR 40k-90k |
| Multi-tender redesign and recurring support | EUR 100k+ |

The commercial argument is direct: this improves win rate, reduces bid friction, protects margin, and makes the contractor easier to underwrite in diligence.

### Why This Is The Strongest Demo

A lawyer immediately understands the premium. This is not abstract AI governance. It is legal and evidentiary structure that helps a client win public contracts.

The client pain is concrete, the buyer is identifiable, the artifact is tangible, and the value can be explained to CEOs, CFOs, bid directors, public procurement lawyers, and PE sponsors.

## 2. Sovereign SaaS / SREN / DORA Moat

### Archetype Definition

A European B2B SaaS provider, headquartered or heavily commercialized in France, selling workflow automation, agentic AI, document processing, KYC/AML tooling, health data tooling, public administration software, or regulated enterprise AI into public sector, healthcare, or financial services.

The strongest version is a private equity-backed or growth-stage SaaS company that must sell into buyers with strict procurement, data sovereignty, operational resilience, and AI accountability requirements.

The opportunity is to turn sovereignty and reversibility into a sales advantage.

### Constraint Stack

| Constraint | Why it matters commercially |
|---|---|
| SREN and sensitive public-sector data hosting | Some public buyers need stronger guarantees around sensitive state data and cloud providers. |
| SecNumCloud posture | French sovereign cloud qualification can become a procurement gate for sensitive workloads. |
| DORA third-party ICT risk | Financial institutions need stronger outsourcing, exit, reversibility, and concentration-risk evidence. |
| AI Act role classification | SaaS architecture can shift obligations depending on whether the vendor is provider, deployer, modifier, or downstream integrator. |
| GDPR and extraterritorial access concerns | Buyers care about data transfers, sub-processors, access paths, and legal control of keys. |

The French SREN law and its April 2026 implementing decree around particularly sensitive state data create a strong sovereignty signal for public-sector cloud services. Source: [economie.gouv.fr](https://www.economie.gouv.fr/daj/publication-du-decret-dapplication-de-larticle-31-de-la-loi-sren-relatif-la-protection-des-donnees-dune-sensibilite-particuliere-de-letat).

DORA creates a parallel procurement blocker for financial entities: ICT third-party risk management, contractual controls, and exit strategies are no longer optional procurement hygiene. Source: [EUR-Lex, Regulation (EU) 2022/2554](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2022/2554/oj).

The EU AI Act also makes high-risk and operator-role classification central to product architecture. The Commission's 2026 simplification communication states that high-risk rules for systems in areas including employment, education, migration, and critical infrastructure apply from 2 December 2027. Source: [European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/eu-agrees-simplify-ai-rules-boost-innovation-and-ban-nudification-apps-protect-citizens).

### Structural Weakness In The Market

Many SaaS vendors still sell a US-centric architecture into European regulated buyers:

- US hyperscaler dependency, even when regionally hosted in Europe
- external API calls to model providers
- vague data residency language
- dynamic sub-processor chains
- weak exit and reversibility documentation
- no operator-role analysis for AI Act purposes
- marketing copy that implies high-risk uses while terms try to disclaim them

This creates procurement delay. The vendor may be functionally attractive, but the buyer's legal, security, and risk teams cannot onboard it quickly.

### Leverage Points

**Sovereign infrastructure posture:** Host sensitive processing on SecNumCloud-qualified or otherwise sovereignty-aligned European infrastructure, with key management and support access controls designed for public-sector review.

**Model and data-flow isolation:** Use local or European model serving where needed, avoid unnecessary external API calls, and document what data enters or leaves each layer.

**DORA reversibility:** Pre-package an exit strategy: deployable containers, export schemas, transition procedures, sub-outsourcing register, service continuity assumptions, and a contractual exit execution schedule.

**AI Act role discipline:** Avoid architectural moves that convert a narrow orchestration layer into a provider of a modified high-risk AI system. Align product docs, sales collateral, technical docs, and terms so the intended use is coherent.

### Opportunity Architecture

| Dimension | Baseline weak structure | Stronger architecture | Strategic upside | Tradeoff |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hosting | Standard public cloud region with broad vendor access | Sovereignty-aligned hosting, strong key control, documented support boundaries | Better public-sector and health-sector eligibility | Higher infra cost and operational complexity |
| Model use | External model APIs receive sensitive workflow data | Local or EU-controlled model layer, RAG, and minimized payloads | Reduced data-transfer and buyer-review friction | More engineering work |
| DORA readiness | Generic data export promise | Tested reversibility playbook, exit agreement, and migration artifacts | Faster financial-sector procurement | Continuous compatibility maintenance |
| Sub-outsourcing | Dynamic global SaaS and API chain | Static, vetted, EU-preferential sub-processor chain | Easier vendor risk review | Less access to convenient developer tools |
| AI Act role | Product marketed broadly with vague disclaimers | Bounded intended use, role matrix, model-change controls, documentation alignment | Lower reclassification and diligence risk | Narrower marketing claims |

### Premium Artifact Shape

The premium deliverable is a Sovereign SaaS Procurement and Reversibility Dossier.

Core sections:

- Sovereignty statement and data-flow map
- Hosting and key-control architecture
- Sub-processor and support-access register
- DORA reversibility playbook
- Exit execution agreement outline
- AI Act operator-role matrix
- Intended-use and marketing alignment review
- Procurement response pack for public and financial-sector buyers

The artifact should be written so the buyer's legal, security, and procurement teams can use it directly in onboarding.

### Monetization Angle

Indicative pricing hypothesis:

| Product | Range |
|---|---|
| Sovereign procurement alignment diagnostic | EUR 50k-120k |
| AI Act operator-role and product-positioning review | EUR 40k-90k |
| DORA onboarding and reversibility package | EUR 75k-200k |

The premium angle is revenue access. The buyer is not paying for a policy binder. The buyer is paying to enter markets where competitors get stuck in legal, cloud, and procurement review.

### Why This Is A Moat

The strongest SaaS vendor is not the one with the longest compliance checklist. It is the one whose architecture makes buyer approval easier.

When sovereignty, reversibility, and role classification are built into the product, the sales cycle changes. Procurement review becomes part of the value proposition rather than a late-stage blocker.

## 3. PE AI Diligence / Vendor Dependency Moat

### Archetype Definition

A private equity sponsor, M&A advisory team, law firm, or operating partner evaluating a European mid-market target with AI-enabled products, AI-assisted operations, or heavy dependency on third-party AI vendors.

The target may be a SaaS company, HR tech company, healthcare workflow vendor, financial software provider, B2B services platform, or tech-enabled services business. The common issue is not "does the company use AI?" The issue is whether AI exposure, vendor dependency, data use, and missing evidence affect valuation, deal terms, closing risk, or post-close remediation cost.

### Constraint Stack

| Constraint | Why it matters commercially |
|---|---|
| Undisclosed AI usage | Management may not have a complete inventory of AI in product, operations, sales, HR, and customer workflows. |
| Vendor dependency | A target may depend on a single model API, ATS, CRM scoring engine, or infrastructure vendor without fallback. |
| Contract misalignment | Customer contracts, vendor terms, DPAs, and product claims may not match actual AI data flows. |
| AI Act classification | A product or workflow may sit near high-risk categories such as employment, credit, education, essential services, or health. |
| Product liability | EU product liability rules now explicitly cover software and AI systems as digital products. |
| Integration risk | Post-close platform consolidation can break assumptions around data, models, and sub-processors. |

The revised EU product liability framework explicitly brings software and AI systems into scope for product-liability analysis. Source: [European Commission](https://commission.europa.eu/news/eu-adapts-product-liability-rules-digital-age-and-circular-economy-2024-12-09_en).

AI systems used in employment and worker management are treated as high-risk under the AI Act's risk logic. Source: [AI Act Service Desk, Recital 57](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/recital-57).

### Structural Weakness In The Market

Standard diligence often misses AI exposure because it is split across workstreams:

- legal reviews contracts
- tech diligence reviews architecture
- cyber reviews security controls
- data protection reviews DPAs
- commercial diligence reviews product claims
- HR diligence reviews workforce processes

AI risk crosses all of them. That means nobody owns the combined picture.

Common hidden issues:

- target uses customer data for model improvement without clear contractual basis
- product claims imply automation or decision support that legal docs do not address
- third-party LLM dependency is operationally critical but not disclosed as a key vendor risk
- high-risk AI category has not been assessed
- human review exists in policy but not in workflow telemetry
- vendor retraining clauses conflict with customer confidentiality or data protection commitments
- post-close migration to a new cloud or model provider would trigger revalidation work

### High-Signal Example: HR Tech And Recruitment AI

Recruitment, candidate screening, employee scoring, and worker management tools are especially strong diligence targets.

Why:

- candidate and employee data is sensitive in practice even when not all fields are special-category data
- automated ranking can have significant individual effects
- human-in-the-loop claims can collapse if workflow data shows recruiters rarely override AI scores
- historical candidate databases may exceed retention expectations
- vendor retraining terms can create purpose-limitation issues
- buyer, insurer, and lender review will care about discrimination and regulatory exposure

This is not a separate archetype for this document. It is the sharpest target vertical inside the PE diligence moat.

### Leverage Points

**Valuation protection:** Convert hidden AI exposure into specific price adjustment, escrow, warranty, covenant, remediation, or walk-away signals.

**Vendor dependency map:** Identify which workflows depend on third-party model providers, AI SaaS vendors, embeddings APIs, data enrichment tools, or opaque scoring systems.

**Contract gap analysis:** Compare product behavior against customer contracts, vendor terms, DPAs, privacy notices, and marketing claims.

**Evidence gap inventory:** Identify records that should exist but do not: AI inventory, ROPA entries, DPIAs, model cards, logs, override records, vendor change notices, sub-processor records, retraining restrictions, and incident history.

**Post-close roadmap:** Give the operating team a 30/60/90-day remediation plan tied to actual deal risk.

### Opportunity Architecture

| Dimension | Baseline weak diligence | Stronger architecture | Strategic upside | Tradeoff |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AI inventory | Management Q&A and generic tech diligence | Function-by-function AI surface map | Fewer hidden exposures at signing | More structured intake |
| Vendor dependency | Key vendors reviewed only by spend | Criticality, reversibility, model-change, and sub-processor mapping | Better valuation and continuity risk view | Needs technical and legal coordination |
| Product claims | Marketing reviewed separately from contracts | Product claims matched against data flows and intended use | Reduces misrepresentation and reclassification risk | Requires cross-workstream synthesis |
| Human oversight | Policy language accepted at face value | Workflow evidence: logs, overrides, latency, reviewer authority | Finds automation bias and fake oversight | Entity-specific data may be needed in later-stage diligence |
| Post-close | Remediation left to portfolio operations | Prioritized AI exposure remediation roadmap | Faster Day-1 control | May create negotiation tension |

### Premium Artifact Shape

The sellable artifact is an AI Exposure Diligence Pack.

Core sections:

- AI surface inventory by product and function
- Critical vendor and model dependency map
- Reversibility and concentration-risk assessment
- Data-flow and sub-processor evidence table
- Contract and product-claim mismatch register
- High-risk category watchlist
- Missing evidence checklist
- Deal-term implications for counsel
- 30/60/90-day post-close remediation roadmap

The artifact should not tell the sponsor what legal conclusion to reach. It should give counsel and investment committee members the evidence needed to ask sharper questions, negotiate better terms, and price the risk.

### Monetization Angle

Indicative pricing hypothesis:

| Product | Range |
|---|---|
| Red-flag AI exposure scan | EUR 15k-35k |
| Full AI diligence pack for a single target | EUR 40k-100k |
| Multi-target sponsor or portfolio review | EUR 100k-250k+ |

The premium angle is enterprise value protection. A small hidden AI dependency can affect retention, customer trust, regulatory cost, warranty exposure, and integration timelines.

### Why This Becomes The Transaction Module

This is the cleanest v2 wedge. It sits naturally inside M&A legal work, technical diligence, cyber diligence, and portfolio operations. It also has higher willingness to pay because the artifact is tied to deal value, not an annual compliance budget.

The first version should stay no-data and archetype-based. Later versions can support controlled entity-specific diligence with counsel-managed intake and strict data boundaries.

## Cross-Archetype Product Logic

These three archetypes share the same product thesis:

| Common pattern | Construction | Sovereign SaaS | PE diligence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Constraint | Public procurement and sustainability evidence | Sovereignty, DORA, AI Act role classification | Hidden AI exposure and vendor dependency |
| Buyer pain | Tender loss, weak award narrative, supplier fragility | Blocked enterprise/public-sector procurement | Valuation leakage and post-close surprises |
| Evidence asset | Tender defensibility dossier | Sovereign procurement and reversibility dossier | AI exposure diligence pack |
| Lawyer role | Interpret procurement rules and contract structure | Interpret regulatory obligations and buyer requirements | Interpret deal implications and legal exposure |
| Client value | Win contracts | Access restricted markets | Protect enterprise value |

This is why the category should remain Exposure Intelligence. The product is not governance. It is structured evidence for commercial decisions where law, technical architecture, and market access overlap.

---

## Recommended Build Sequence

1. Build the French construction procurement demo first.
2. Use the Sovereign SaaS archetype as the advanced technical-legal demo for SaaS founders, PE software teams, and digital-law boutiques.
3. Package PE AI diligence as the transaction module once the first artifact has partner validation.

The construction archetype should be the first public-facing premium demo because its value is easiest to understand without explaining the entire AI Act. The SaaS archetype is stronger for expert buyers. The PE archetype is strongest for high-ticket expansion.
