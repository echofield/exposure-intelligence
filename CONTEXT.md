# Exposure Intelligence — Vertical Context

> Layer: operational context
> Version: 1.0
> Audience: any session building within this project

---

## 1. Category

This is **exposure intelligence infrastructure**, not AI governance consulting.

The distinction is structural:

- AI governance consulting sells methodology, frameworks, and workshops.
- Exposure intelligence infrastructure produces a **standardized, repeatable technical artifact** that legal advisors use as an evidence instrument.

The artifact is the product. The engine that produces it is invisible.

---

## 2. Vertical thesis

European companies are buying **auditable evidence work** around AI — not abstract governance.

Near-term demand clusters:
- AI use-case inventories and classification
- AI Act readiness and risk tiering
- Vendor and third-party AI dependency review
- Evidence generation for boards, legal teams, procurement, and compliance
- AI due diligence in M&A and investment contexts

Strongest positioning:

> **Technical evidence infrastructure for trusted advisory workflows.**

Legal advisory is the first distribution wedge, not the final category.

---

## 3. No-data architecture

v1 runs on **client archetypes**, never on real client data.

Input: sector, jurisdiction, company size, AI surface category, deployment model.

No raw client data. No HR records. No customer files. No dataroom ingestion. No source-code scanning. No model ingestion. No production integrations.

Why this matters:
- No data processor liability
- No security or procurement burden on the law firm
- No deployment friction — the artifact can be initiated from a single intake form
- The product promise is not "we know everything inside your company." It is: "we produce a structured exposure artifact your legal advisor can use immediately."

This is not a limitation. It is the competitive wedge against heavy scanning platforms and hybrid SaaS tools that require technical integration before producing any output.

---

## 4. Archetype model

Every artifact run begins with an archetype — a structured input across 10 dimensions:

1. Jurisdiction
2. Sector
3. Company size
4. Revenue band
5. Employee band
6. AI surface category
7. Business function affected
8. Deployment model
9. Regulatory sensitivity
10. External dependency level

Same archetype → same artifact shape. Repeatability is the structural guarantee of the product.

See `archetypes/SCHEMA.md` for the full specification.

---

## 5. Distribution logic

Primary channel: boutique law firms, white-label, under their letterhead.

The artifact sits beneath the legal opinion. The law firm does not need to explain the methodology — they trust the structure and use the output.

Common law-firm models this fits into:
- Back-office technical services under the firm's client relationship
- Third-party assessment reports embedded into legal opinions or due diligence memos
- White-label under the firm's brand
- Strategic alliance or co-branded delivery

The distribution risk: law firms may like the product and still not sell it. The meeting test is not "do you like it?" It is:

> "Would you bill this under your name to a client in the next 90 days?"

If yes: build pilot. If no: use the lawyer for language validation, not distribution.

## 7. Vocabulary firewall

Two layers, strictly separated:

**Internal (engine layer):** MSAE, NCE, IGE, CAE, IRA, FRA, ramification, forced futures, negative-space, propagation boundaries, constraint architecture, incentive geometry, narrative compression. These terms live in `PROTOCOL.md` only.

**Client-facing (artifact layer):** Plain language. "Structured technical AI risk evidence supporting legal advisory." No internal terms appear in any artifact, template, archetype file, or client communication.

Anything a lawyer or their client sees uses the client-facing register only.

---

## 8. Competitive position

Three incumbent architectures and why this product sits outside all of them:

| Architecture | Strength | Weakness vs. this product |
|---|---|---|
| Heavy in-place scanning (DSPM / data discovery platforms) | Deep visibility into live environments | High deployment friction, not law-firm compatible, requires IT procurement |
| Hybrid SaaS + local scanners (GRC platforms) | Strong workflow and case management | Requires internal access, looks like software procurement, not an advisory artifact |
| Assessment / metadata / evidence-only governance tools | Lower data risk, closer architecture | Becomes questionnaire commodity without structural differentiation |

This product's wedge: archetype-based, no-data-ingestion, standardized artifact, law-firm native.

---

## 9. What not to build in v1

- Generic AI governance dashboard
- Data ingestion or integration layer
- Policy template vendor offering
- Workshop-only delivery
- Per-partner artifact customization
- Any frontend before the artifact has commercial pull
- Annual partner SaaS before proving repeatability
